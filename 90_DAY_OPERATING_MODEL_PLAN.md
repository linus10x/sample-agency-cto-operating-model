# SAMPLE: first 90 days as a fractional agency CTO

> **Illustrative sample by Kunjar Bhaduri, Bhaduri Advisory. Fictional organization and invented evidence only. No client relationship or confidential engagement material. Prepared with AI drafting assistance under my direction. Revised October 2, 2026 (America/Chicago).**

## Mandate and working assumptions

The fictional agency needs senior technical judgment in presales, dependable delivery, internal accelerators and effective technology partnerships. Those demands compete for the same people. I would make that allocation visible, improve the handoffs and use the evidence to choose an operating model with the existing leaders.

Assume 2-3 fractional days a week (16-24 hours) for 13 weeks, with CEO sponsorship, a delivery lead, a solutions/presales lead and finance support. Leadership interviews and records need to be available in week one. The CTO's capacity covers diagnosis, selected solution reviews, coaching and governance. Delivery managers and engineering teams own implementation. Large restructuring or additional presales volume needs a separate capacity decision.

## Days 1-10: learn quickly and make reversible improvements

Meet the leaders closest to active customer commitments, delivery and commercial decisions. Inspect three current projects and five recent estimates first, then expand the sample to ten pitches as records permit. Identify deadlines, delivery risks, unowned decisions and proposals awaiting engineering input. Fix an obvious handoff or owner gap immediately with the relevant leader; do not wait a month for a structural recommendation.

Start a four-week view of senior time across delivery, presales, internal initiatives and partner work. Use available timesheets and interviews, recording missing or inconsistent data. Agree a small decision log and an urgent escalation route with the CEO.

## Days 11-30: establish a baseline and propose options

Review how scope, assumptions, estimates and commercial terms move from sales to delivery. With finance, reconcile project revenue, labor cost, write-offs and changes. Review the work split by onshore, nearshore and offshore teams, including overlap hours, review ownership and client data/access boundaries. For each major technology partner, identify actual pipeline, obligations, technical support and a named owner.

The day-30 readout gives the CEO observed strengths, material problems, evidence limits, reversible fixes already made and the recommended model to pilot. It includes the capacity and margin implications. The recommendation follows the evidence; no organizational option is predetermined.

| Option | Decision rights | Fits when | Tradeoff to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Practice leads | Practice lead owns technical quality and solution approval; delivery lead owns project execution | Capabilities are distinct and each has sufficient scale | Cross-practice work needs a single accountable solution owner |
| Delivery/solutions split | Solutions lead owns design/estimate; delivery lead accepts execution; finance accepts commercial assumptions | Presales volume and estimate handoffs are the main constraint | The acceptance gate must prevent unowned delivery commitments |
| Client/partner pods | Pod lead owns execution; central architecture lead owns shared standards | A few substantial accounts dominate demand | Shared specialists and cross-pod standards need explicit allocation |

## Days 31-60: pilot the model on selected work

Choose two suitable engagements with their client/delivery owners. Apply these mechanisms before changing titles:

* **Estimate acceptance.** Delivery owner approves scope, assumptions, dependencies, exclusions and capacity. Finance approves cost/margin assumptions; the commercial owner approves terms. A change after acceptance is versioned and re-reviewed. An urgent exception has a named executive accepting the risk.
* **Protected internal capacity.** CEO approves a weekly budget for one selected accelerator with a product owner and acceptance criteria. Track actual use and expected client value. If a live customer emergency displaces it, record the decision and reschedule rather than pretending the budget was protected.
* **Delivery handoff.** A single accountable owner accepts code, tests, deployment notes, access/data controls and outstanding defects. Agree time-zone overlap, response windows and escalation. The receiving team demonstrates it can deploy and support the work. The checklist is shared across locations; it does not assume identical local context.

Coach the involved leaders in the decisions they own. Review estimate changes, handoff defects and partner commitments weekly. The CEO decides whether the pilot justifies a broader model after day 60.

## Worked fictional decision: estimate, margin and scarce senior time

Assume a fixed-scope project has $60,000 revenue and a $60/hour direct delivery cost (fictional loaded team labor cost, not my consulting rate). Version 1 estimates 500 hours: $30,000 cost and 50% gross margin. A comparable completed scenario used 650 hours: $39,000 cost, 35% margin and 30% effort overrun. That is a diagnostic example, not an achieved agency result. The delivery owner identifies missing integration/test work and accepts a revised 590-hour estimate, including that work and contingency. Expected direct cost is $35,400 and expected margin is 41%, against a fictional finance-approved 40% floor. All margins exclude overhead and other costs; finance must use a consistent basis.

The same fictional team has 80 senior hours/week available. Committed delivery needs 60, requested presales needs 25 and the proposed internal budget needs 8: demand is 93 hours, exceeding capacity by 13. The CEO limits senior presales to 12 hours by prioritizing selected proposals and deferring the rest, so 60 + 12 + 8 = 80. This is an explicit tradeoff, not an assumption of unpaid overtime or added staff. It supports piloting Option B because design/estimate responsibility must transfer to an accountable delivery owner while the solutions lead manages the constrained presales queue.

Acceptance version 2 records scope, the 590-hour baseline, capacity and finance approval. If the client adds 60 hours without price change, version 3 becomes 650 hours, $39,000 direct cost and 35% expected margin. Delivery and finance require a scope/price change or a named CEO exception before the commercial owner commits. At the 40% floor, revenue would need to be at least $65,000 ($39,000 / 0.60), subject to negotiation and agreed cost basis. Record the decision and reforecast against the accepted version; do not report version-3 hours as unexplained delivery overrun against version 2.

## Days 61-90: adopt what worked and hand over

Expand the tested mechanisms where the evidence supports it. Publish decision rights and escalation paths, the accepted model and capacity plan. Agree a senior owner and a quarterly working review for each material partner, with client-value and delivery results rather than partnership status alone. Give the CEO a hiring/leadership recommendation with the scope, cost and transition conditions for a permanent CTO, a promoted leader or continued fractional support.

Day-90 acceptance requires two sampled projects using the agreed estimate and handoff gates, an explained capacity/margin baseline, a delivery exception log and leaders able to operate the process without the fractional CTO attending every meeting. If the pilot has not produced that evidence, continue or revise it instead of declaring the model adopted.

## Monthly measures and decision use

| Measure | Definition and owner | Decision it informs |
|---|---|---|
| Senior capacity | Hours by category / total available senior hours; operations lead; record unallocated and missing hours | Rebalance sales support, delivery and internal work |
| Win rate | Wins / decided qualified proposals in the cohort; commercial lead; exclude open proposals | Review where technical involvement improves conversion |
| Forecast error | (Actual delivery hours - approved baseline hours) / baseline hours, only for positive baselines; delivery lead | Improve assumptions; explain scope changes separately |
| Project gross margin | (Recognized project revenue - agreed direct delivery cost) / revenue, only for positive revenue; finance; record cost basis | Evaluate commercial and delivery choices consistently |
| Handoff quality | Escaped defects by severity per accepted handoff; quality lead; show sample size/context | Improve the transfer; do not rank locations on raw counts |
| Internal work | Accepted increments against approved scope/time/capacity; product owner | Continue, reduce or stop an accelerator based on use/value |

Targets follow the baseline and CEO agreement. This sample contains no actual agency measurements and makes no claim that a particular model raises win rate or margin. It is intended for agency CTO mandates; fit to a specific role must be checked against that role's actual description.
