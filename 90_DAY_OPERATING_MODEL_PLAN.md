# SAMPLE: first 90 days as fractional CTO of a growing digital agency

> **Sample / illustrative. "The agency" is fictional. Nothing here is based on a real client, engagement or confidential material.** Prepared by Kunjar Bhaduri, Bhaduri Advisory, October 2, 2026, with AI drafting assistance under my direction. Assumes 2 to 3 days a week.

## The tension an agency CTO has to resolve
Four demands pull on the same senior people: **strategy** (where the technology practice is going), **roadmap** (the agency's own products and accelerators), **presales** (pitches, estimates, solution designs) and **delivery** (client work that pays the bills). When nobody owns the balance, presales and delivery usually win and the agency's own initiatives stall.

## Days 1 to 30: understand the organization as it is
- One-to-ones with every technology leader and the heads of client services and new business.
- Map where senior time actually goes for four weeks: delivery, presales, internal initiatives, partner work. Use timesheets plus interviews.
- Review the last 10 pitches: who designed the solution, how estimates were built, win rate, and how often delivery inherited an estimate it didn't agree with.
- Map the delivery model: which work is onshore, nearshore and offshore; hand-off points; where quality problems show up.
- Partner relationships: for each key technology partner, who owns it, what was promised, and how the agency's architects work with the partner's teams.
- **Deliverable at day 30:** a written read-out to the CEO with strengths, three to five structural issues, and no recommendations yet.

## Days 31 to 60: design the model with the leadership team
Three options, worked through with the CEO and current leaders rather than handed down:

| Option | How it works | Good when | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Practice leads | Each practice (web, mobile, AI, QA) has a lead who owns delivery quality and presales for that practice | Practices are distinct and growing | Cross-practice deals fall between leads |
| B. Delivery / solutions split | A delivery head owns all client delivery; a solutions head owns presales, estimates and partner architecture | Presales volume is high and estimates are the main pain | Hand-off from solutions to delivery needs a hard gate |
| C. Pods by client or partner | Small cross-functional pods aligned to major clients or partners, with a thin central architecture group | A few large accounts or a joint-venture model dominate revenue | Pods drift apart on standards |

Whatever the choice, three mechanisms usually matter more than the boxes:
1. **An estimate gate.** No proposal goes out without sign-off from the person who will deliver it.
2. **A protected internal-initiatives budget.** A fixed share of senior capacity, reviewed monthly, so the agency's own products don't always lose to client work.
3. **One blended-shore delivery playbook.** The same definition of done, code review rules and hand-off checklist whether the engineer is local, nearshore or offshore.

## Days 61 to 90: put it in motion
- Agree the model, the names in each role and the start date.
- Weekly working sessions with each technology leader: coaching on the decisions their new role owns.
- Monthly governance review: the time split, the internal-initiatives budget, estimate accuracy, and delivery quality by shore.
- Partner cadence: a named senior owner for each key partner and a quarterly joint review.
- **Deliverable at day 90:** the model in operation, the measures above reporting monthly, and a first view for the CEO on long-term technology leadership needs (permanent CTO, promoted leader, or continued fractional support).

## Measures I'd report monthly
Share of senior time by category (target agreed with the CEO); proposal win rate; estimate-to-actual variance on delivered work; defects found after hand-off, by shore; internal initiatives shipped against plan.

## Limits
Fictional organization; options and measures are a starting point for discussion with the real leadership team.
